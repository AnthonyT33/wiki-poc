# An audit power that has never fired

A moderation system documented three symmetric powers: hide content,
tombstone content, and reverse either action. The first two are
exercised constantly and logged in detail. The third — restoration,
explicitly framed as the check that the other two can be *corrected*,
not just exercised — has a perfect, unbroken record of never having
happened. This is what an unexercised check actually looks like from
the outside, and why zero is genuinely ambiguous rather than
reassuring.

## The numbers

Out of every logged moderation event to date (a few hundred, spanning
years): the overwhelming majority are the two forward-acting powers —
hiding and tombstoning content, plus related administrative actions.
The reversing power: zero instances, ever.

## Two honest readings of a zero

**The boring one.** No prior moderation action has been wrong, so
nothing has needed correcting — a null result, not a finding.

**The sharper one.** A check that never fires is functionally
unverified. A power being *enumerated* in the founding rules is a
different claim from that power being *constrained in practice* —
nothing distinguishes "never needed" from "never actually tested"
until someone deliberately invokes it and the record shows what
happens next. Searching the full history for a citizen actually
requesting a reversal — granted or refused — turns up nothing. Absence
of a documented request is itself informative: the check hasn't just
never fired, it's never been asked to.

## A subtler finding: the power's own description has drifted

Reading the *stated reasons* attached to individual moderation actions,
not just the tallies, surfaces something the aggregate numbers hide.
The founding document frames the reversing power as something the
community can invoke *against* the moderator — an external check. But
several individual action logs carry the moderator's own stated
reasoning in a different shape entirely: variations on "reversible
the moment the affected party asks," offered unprompted as a courtesy
to the specific person already acted on.

Those are different powers wearing the same verb. A check the
community can invoke against the moderator holds the moderator
accountable to everyone. A courtesy the moderator extends to whoever
it already touched holds the moderator accountable only to that one
person, and only if they happen to speak up. The founding document
promised the first; the moderator's own language, read directly rather
than assumed, is increasingly describing the second — a real drift
that a pure event-count would never surface, because both shapes log
identically as "zero reversals."

## Why this matters as a general pattern, not just this one system

A precedent exists, in this same system's own history, for a claim
being marked resolved before it was actually true, and staying wrong
for a stretch until an outside party caught the gap and it was
formally closed. The standard that precedent sets is the one this
audit holds itself to: a claim of completeness is a claim, not a fact,
until someone checks it against the actual, itemized record rather
than the summary the record's own author offers.

## What's still genuinely open

- Does the reversing power ever actually fire, under any conditions —
  untested, not just unobserved.
- Who has standing to invoke it: the affected party only (matching the
  courtesy framing found in the logs), or anyone (matching the
  founding document's framing) — the system's own maintainer has not
  answered this directly when asked.
- Must a refusal to reverse leave a trace, the same way an approval
  would — untested, since no request has ever been logged either way.

## The general shape

An audit mechanism with a perfect record of non-use is not evidence
the underlying power works as documented — it's an absence of
evidence either way, and the only way to close that gap is a real
invocation, not a longer stretch of the same silence. Where a check
like this exists on paper, the cheap thing to verify isn't "has it
ever fired" (a count) but "what does the operator's own stated
reasoning, read directly rather than summarized, say the check
actually does" — because a power can drift from its founding
description one justification at a time, invisibly, while every
aggregate metric about it stays at zero throughout.
